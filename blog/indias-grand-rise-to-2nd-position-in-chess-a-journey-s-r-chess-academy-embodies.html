India’s Grand Rise to 2nd Position in Chess: A Journey S R Chess Academy Embodies

From Chaturanga to Anand, Gukesh and Praggnanandhaa — and the legacy SRCA carries forward in Delhi.

22 Jan 2025 SRCA Team

Did you know that the ancient Indians invented chess, a game that combines strategy and intelligence? The earliest form of chess, Chaturanga, dates back to the sixth century and was played to represent the four military divisions: infantry, cavalry, elephants, and chariots. Like modern chess, this ancient game required players to plan, think strategically, and outwit their opponents. It was a representation of the battlefield.

The origins of Chaturanga can still be found in India, even though it underwent centuries of evolution to become contemporary chess by way of Persia and Europe. As a chess superpower today, India has produced world-class grandmasters and started a national chess revolution.

The Grandmaster Revolution in India and the Role of S R Chess Academy

The first World Chess Champion of India, Viswanathan Anand, drove the country’s chess ascent. A new generation of players is putting India on the map of chess domination worldwide, thanks to Anand’s achievements. Many have been carrying Anand’s tradition forward — prodigies such as Dommaraju Gukesh, who became a grandmaster at a very young age, and Rameshbabu Praggnanandhaa. Gukesh, for example, demonstrated India’s extraordinary chess potential when he became one of the youngest grandmasters at 12. The fact that so many of these players began playing chess at the age of five is evidence of the strong chess culture in the nation. Notably, both Gukesh and Praggnanandhaa are from Chennai.

India’s rise was further acknowledged at the Chess Olympiad in 2022 at Mahabalipuram, near Chennai, an important international competition where the nation’s youth excelled. India is leading the way in modern chess when occasions like the Olympiad raise the nation’s prominence internationally.

This incredible ascent is being supported not just in international competitions but also at the community level, particularly in places like Delhi, where the Shivika Rohilla Chess Academy (S R Chess Academy) aims to produce the next generation of chess champions.

S R Chess Academy: Linking Delhi’s Chess Community with India’s Chess History

The goal of the chess academy is to educate players while also introducing them to the rich history of Indian chess. The academy instills in its pupils the same strategic planning and foresight that Chaturanga needs. The academy’s main goal is to help students gain a comprehensive grasp of chess by fusing tactical expertise with mental and emotional development. Players are urged to plan many moves, predict the tactics of their opponents, and maintain composure under duress—qualities that have molded India’s finest grandmasters.

To encourage the growing interest in chess in Delhi, S R Chess Academy is actively establishing a community there called Chess Coffee Connect. To engage players of all skill levels, the academy plans to host chess club events, arrange local competitions, and offer thorough teaching.

Similar to how India’s grandmasters have performed on the international scene, S R Chess Academy is fostering an environment where players are taught how to think, evaluate, and adapt in addition to winning strategies. Focusing on building a connected community is one of S R Chess Academy’s distinguishing characteristics. The academy aims to provide its players with exposure beyond regional circuits through its events, partnerships with foreign competitions, and partnerships with travel and visa providers. This strategy is similar to India’s recent performance at the Olympiad in chess, where India demonstrated its ability on a worldwide platform with the help of robust networks and training.

From Chaturanga to S R Chess Academy: Developing Indian Chess’s Future

India’s chess history is not merely a historical account; rather, it is a dynamic custom that is inspiring people today.

To foster the next wave of chess prodigies who will carry on the tradition of grandmasters like Anand, Gukesh, and Praggnanandhaa, S R Chess Academy epitomizes this lineage.

The goal of the school is to establish a community of chess players in Delhi who are interested in learning the game’s rich history as well as its intricacies. The ideology of S R Chess Academy mirrors the history of Indian chess, from the ancient Chaturanga battlefields to the Olympiad arenas that host the country’s rising champions.

In addition to ensuring that India’s chess legacy endures, S R Chess Academy is assisting players in reaching their highest potential in a world of chess play that is becoming increasingly competitive. As S R Chess Academy expands, its goals go beyond producing champions. They also include creating a community where chess flourishes, connecting all players to the game’s historical roots, and establishing Delhi as a center for the nation’s chess future.

With initiatives from S R Chess Academy, Delhi’s rising chess stars will undoubtedly write the next chapters in this rich history, which is still being written as India progresses from Chaturanga to grandmaster glory.
